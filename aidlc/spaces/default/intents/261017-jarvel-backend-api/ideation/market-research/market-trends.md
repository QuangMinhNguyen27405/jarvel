# Market Trends: Jarvel

Read alongside the approved intent statement (`ideation/intent-capture/intent-statement.md`), which targets public users who want safety, a relaxed pace, public transport and a cost estimate.

## Trends

| Trend | Evidence | What it means for Jarvel |
|-------|----------|--------------------------|
| AI trip planners are a crowded category | 2026 roundups compare 5 to 15 AI planners each ([Lindy](https://www.lindy.ai/blog/trip-planner-app), [ToolWorthy](https://www.toolworthy.ai/blog/best-ai-travel-planner-tools), [Stippl](https://docs.stippl.io/blog/best-ai-travel-planner-2026)) | "AI writes your itinerary" alone is not a reason to switch; the safety and pace guarantees must carry the difference (Answer Q2) |
| Planners move towards booking | Mindtrip and Layla described as generate-and-book tools ([Lindy](https://www.lindy.ai/blog/trip-planner-app), [Unite.ai](https://www.unite.ai/?p=319272)) | Booking links are table stakes (Answer Q3); full booking is not in Jarvel's chosen difference |
| Safety scoring by neighbourhood is mainstream | TripIt added neighbourhood safety scores; GeoSure scores day vs night worldwide ([Techlicious](https://www.techlicious.com/blog/tripit-neighborhood-safety-scores-international-travel-tips), [We3Travel](https://we3travel.com/?p=120967)) | Travelers already expect safety information; Jarvel's step is to apply it inside the plan, hour by hour |
| Crime-aware routing apps rely on open police data | ZoneScout and SafeRoute use police or city crime data ([App Store: ZoneScout](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6760105754), [Product Hunt listing: SafeRoute](https://webmail.hunted.space/product/saferoute)) | Open crime data exists in some places (US and UK named), not everywhere; supports the intent's rule to warn where safety was not checked |
| Login walls before output | MonkeyTravel reports Mindtrip and Layla ask for an account first (competitor claim, [MonkeyTravel](https://monkeytravel.app/pt/blog/mindtrip-alternative-2026)) | Jarvel also requires sign-up (approved intent); not a differentiator either way |

## Customer Expectations

- Table stakes: map of each day, opening hours checked, booking links, sharing with companions (Answer Q3).
- Differentiators Jarvel will compete on: hourly safety, rules-enforced relaxed pace, transit-first with last-train awareness, cost estimate (Answer Q2).

## Rules and Shifts to Watch

| Area | Why it matters | Source |
|------|----------------|--------|
| Privacy laws for user data (for example GDPR, CCPA) | Public sign-up stores personal data, and "anyone online" may include EU and California residents | Answer Q6, Q7 |
| Responsibility for safety advice | The planner tells people which areas to avoid and when; wrong advice could harm someone, so disclaimers and wording matter | Answer Q7 |
| Terms of use of outside data | Place, map, transit and crime information will come from outside sources (Answer Q8), each with its own usage and attribution terms | Answer Q7, Q8 |

## Audience

- First users: anyone who finds it online (Answer Q6), with fewer than 100 expected in the first three months (approved intent statement).
- Market size (TAM/SAM/SOM) was not estimated: the approved goal is a small free launch, so sizing would not change any decision now.

## Assumptions & Open Questions

- Hypothesis: travelers who already use safety scores would value having them applied inside an itinerary. No traveler has been asked yet.
- The trend evidence is mostly vendor-written content from 2026; no independent market report was consulted.
